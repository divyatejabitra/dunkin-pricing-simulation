# Speaker Notes — Retail Coffee Market: Competitive Landscape

For the 9/24 preliminary market analysis. 8 content slides + 1 cover, ~15 minutes total
(~100 seconds/slide). Visual deck: `campus_coffee_map.html` (open in a browser and present
from there, or use as a reference to rebuild in PowerPoint/Google Slides).

**Read this first:** every price on every slide is marked "est." because it comes from
third-party menu-aggregator sites, not a verified in-store/app price — same caveat that
applied to our original 4-vendor pricing work. Confirm real prices before this goes in
front of the client. See `data/macro_competitors.csv` for the exact source per number.

---

## Cover
"We're presenting the competitive landscape Dunkin' would enter at River Campus — six
competitors, what they charge for the exact products in question, who already has a campus
foothold, and two slides on the broader market and how price sensitivity actually breaks
down across student and non-student segments."

## 1. Starbucks
- Already inside Wilson Commons — this is the incumbent Dunkin' is displacing, not a
  hypothetical rival.
- Price: ~$5.95 large iced latte, ~$3.75 large hot coffee (both est.)
- Say explicitly: Starbucks sets the campus price *ceiling* and the habit (mobile order +
  Rewards) Dunkin' has to interrupt.

## 2. Peet's Coffee
- Also already on campus (Wegmans Hall) — proves two coffee options can coexist here.
- Price: ~$5.95 large iced latte, from ~$3.40 large hot coffee (both est.)
- The interesting point isn't the price, it's the *awareness gap*: Peet's is priced close
  to Starbucks but has far less brand recognition. Suggests visibility, not just price,
  drives share on this campus.

## 3. Tim Hortons
- Dunkin's closest national positioning rival — same "everyday value" segment.
- Price: $2.49 large hot coffee (confirmed), ~$4.19 latte (medium only — large unconfirmed,
  flag this verbally)
- The real finding: Tim Hortons has **documented, real campus locations** at University at
  Buffalo — same region as Rochester — and UB even made them the "Official Coffee of UB
  Athletics." This isn't hypothetical; it's proof a value-coffee chain can win a campus
  contract exactly like the one being pitched here.

## 4. Dutch Bros Coffee
- No real in-building campus presence, but wins the demographic Dunkin' most needs:
  under-25 customers.
- Price: ~$6.75 large iced latte, ~$3.75 large hot drink (both est., wide regional range)
- The lesson isn't the price — it's that 80% customization and TikTok/Instagram culture
  drive loyalty with this age group more than price does. Worth a beat on whether Dunkin'
  can borrow any of that playbook.

## 5. McDonald's McCafé
- Sets the bottom of the price range Dunkin' competes across.
- Price: ~$3.19 large iced coffee (confirmed at that size); basic hot coffee as low as
  ~$1–1.49 (large size not itemized — flag this)
- Say: "Dunkin' has to price above McCafé and explain why — 'cheaper than McDonald's' isn't
  a selling point Dunkin' can use, because McCafé already owns that position."

## 6. Independent coffee shops near River Campus
- Equal Grounds, Java's, Ugly Duck, Layali, Winter Swan, Albunn, Mercury.
- No pricing data yet — say so plainly, don't guess a number here.
- The point: these are the real off-campus substitute for students who already live off
  campus, chosen for atmosphere/study-spot appeal rather than price. They're the backdrop
  Dunkin' should not mistake for its actual competitive set on-campus.

## 7. Market context: size, growth, who's drinking more
- US branded coffee shop market: ~$58.5B, +4.2% last year, 45,200+ outlets, 588 brands
  (fragmented — chains ~70-75% of revenue, independents ~25-30%)
- 66% of US adults drank coffee yesterday; specialty coffee at a record 47%
- 18-24-year-olds: 46% had specialty coffee yesterday; coffee use in the 18-26 age group is
  up 30%+ since 2020 — the fastest-growing segment in the whole category
- Say: "The growth engine of this market right now is exactly the population on this
  campus — that's the demand-side case for entering at all."

## 8. Price sensitivity isn't just about age
- General trend: rising prices are pushing the broader population toward home brewing,
  fewer shop visits (NCA, 2026)
- Counterintuitive finding worth flagging out loud: some campus studies find full-time
  students report a *higher* self-reported willingness to pay/budget for coffee than
  working adults in the same study — the "students are cheap" assumption doesn't hold
  universally
- The real difference isn't willingness to pay — it's *what* students are optimizing for:
  frequency/habit and payment friction (dining dollars vs. cash) may matter as much as the
  sticker price
- This is the bridge back to our survey: the payment-method question (Q12 in the Google
  Forms) exists specifically to test this. Close on: "price alone may not decide this —
  matching students' existing payment method may matter just as much as beating Starbucks
  by 25 cents."
