## How to approach someone

1. Ask: *"Do you have 2 minutes for a quick class survey about coffee on campus? It's
   anonymous."*
2. Hand them your phone/laptop with **the correct version's link open** per the table above,
   or read the questions aloud if they prefer.
3. Don't mention that the price changes between versions, and don't tell them what price
   other respondents saw - that would bias their answer.
4. Let them answer every question themselves if possible; only read it aloud if asked.
5. Mark the row done, move to the next respondent, open the next version's link.

## Who to survey

- Any current UofR student, faculty, or staff member who drinks hot coffee or lattes at
  least occasionally (the Form screens this automatically - if someone is screened out,
  it doesn't count toward your 10; go find another respondent).
- Spread across people you don't already know well if you can - a mix of undergrads,
  grad students, and (if possible) staff/faculty makes the pooled dataset more useful, since
  the research question is specifically about how price sensitivity differs by group.
- Avoid surveying only your own friend group or major - that skews the segmentation data.

## If something goes wrong

- **Respondent gets screened out (not UofR, or doesn't drink coffee):** thank them, don't
  count it, find someone else for that slot.
- **You lose track of which version is next:** count how many *completed* responses you
  have so far (check the Form's response count if unsure) and resume the rotation at that
  number + 1.
- **Someone wants to do it on paper instead of a phone:** print the matching version's
  question set from `version_A_discount.txt` / `_B_match.txt` / `_C_premium.txt` and enter
  their answers into the correct Form afterward yourself.
